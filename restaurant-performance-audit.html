Restaurant Performance Audit

A structured review for restaurant owners who need to understand where profitability is being lost—and what to address first.

When a performance audit makes sense

Your restaurant can be busy while cash remains tight. The cause might be a costing assumption, an operating habit or several small issues acting together. An audit is a way to establish a baseline before making broad changes to prices, portions or staffing.

Kitchen Pilot looks at the relationship between food costs, menu contribution and day-to-day operations. The purpose is to distinguish what the available evidence supports from what still needs to be measured.

What the review covers

  • Up to 30 core menu items: recipe ingredients, standard portions, usable yields, current purchase costs and selling prices.
  • Food-cost variance: available inventory and purchasing records compared with the cost implied by recipes and sales.
  • Operating pressure points: waste, stock handling, purchasing decisions, labour deployment, prep and service workflow.
  • Corrective action: prioritized findings, practical changes and measures to review after implementation.

How the process works

  1. Define the question. Discuss the symptoms, the restaurant’s priorities and which information exists. Confirm scope and fees before beginning.
  2. Build a consistent picture. Align dates, units, recipes and sales categories so the comparisons are meaningful. Flag missing or uncertain inputs.
  3. Investigate the gaps. Connect the numbers with how products are ordered, prepared, portioned and sold. A variance is a reason to investigate, not proof of one particular cause.
  4. Choose the next actions. Separate immediate controls from changes that need more observation or better records. Agree what to measure to assess progress.

What to have ready

A menu, recent supplier invoices, recipes and portion standards, item-level sales, inventory counts and relevant labour records provide a useful starting point. If some are missing, say so in your enquiry. The available records affect the scope and confidence of the findings.
