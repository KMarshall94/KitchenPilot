Restaurant operations consulting

When a team works hard but service still slows down, look at how the work flows—not just how many people are on the schedule.

Find where service gets held up

A long ticket time is an outcome. The cause may begin earlier: an incomplete prep list, equipment shared by several stations, unclear handoffs or an item whose preparation does not fit peak demand. Adding labour without identifying the constraint may leave the original problem in place.

Kitchen Pilot’s diagnostic approach considers the sequence of work, available operating information and the realities of your menu. The aim is practical corrective action that your team can understand and evaluate.

Questions that guide an operations review

  • Before service: Are prep quantities based on demand? Are priorities and station responsibilities clear?
  • During service: Where do tickets queue? Which tasks compete for equipment or workspace? Are handoffs causing rework?
  • Labour deployment: Do skills and shift overlap match the workload? Is time being spent moving, searching or duplicating work?
  • After service: Are waste, incomplete prep and recurring delays recorded in a way that informs the next shift?

Read labour efficiency in context

Labour cost as a share of sales can change because sales fall, wage costs change or scheduled hours do not match demand. The ratio alone does not tell you which happened. Compare similar periods and consider opening hours, preparation requirements, menu mix and service expectations before making staffing decisions.

Cutting hours is not automatically the right answer. A process change may reduce duplicated effort; a different overlap may help the busiest station. The evidence should determine the recommendation.

Remote analysis and on-site observation

Remote work can review schedules, preparation routines, ticket information and your team’s description of the process. It cannot directly observe every movement in a kitchen. If a finding depends on observation, that limitation should be explicit.

On-site support is available in Wasaga Beach, Barrie and Collingwood, Ontario. A visit’s focus, timing and fee are agreed before work begins. Compare remote and on-site support.

Connect the operation to the menu

Workflow and profitability are linked. A dish with a useful ingredient contribution may still put pressure on scarce equipment during peak service. Review menu profitability alongside operating requirements rather than judging either in isolation.
