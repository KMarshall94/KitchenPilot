Restaurant calculators

Use the same reporting period and one currency throughout each calculation. Sales should exclude sales tax and reflect discounts and refunds. These tools calculate your inputs; they do not tell you whether the inputs or targets suit your restaurant.

By Kitchen Pilot Consulting · Updated September 23, 2026

Calculations run in your browser. Inputs are not sent or saved by these tools. Dollar results use the currency you entered. Read the profitability guide for interpretation.

Prime cost calculator

(Food and beverage cost + labour cost) ÷ net sales × 100

Use burdened labour and inventory-based cost of goods for the same period.

Food cost calculator

(Opening inventory + net purchases − closing inventory) ÷ net food sales × 100

Simple calculation with no transfers. Adjust inputs consistently for transfers and use net purchases after credits.

Labour cost calculator

Labour cost ÷ net sales × 100; net sales ÷ worked hours

Include employer costs. Hours and sales must cover the same period and roles.

Break-even sales calculator

Fixed costs ÷ (1 − variable cost percentage ÷ 100)

Classify all costs once. Include genuinely variable labour in the variable ratio, fixed salaries in fixed costs. Assumes stable mix and costs within operating capacity; excludes loan principal and cash timing.

Menu pricing calculator

Ingredient cost ÷ (target food cost percentage ÷ 100)

This is a starting price based only on ingredients. Check demand, guest value, other variable costs, labour and overhead before setting a selling price.

Check a worked example

With $80,000 sales, $25,600 food and beverage cost and $28,000 labour, prime cost is $53,600 (67%). With $30,000 fixed costs and 60% variable costs, break-even sales are $75,000. A $6 ingredient cost at a 30% target implies a $20 selling price and $14 left after ingredients, before other costs. These are illustrative figures, not targets.
